## Everything wrong for a person, ranked

### R1 — LOW · Changing the text size leaves the composer field 2 px shorter than its own buttons until the next keystroke

**Where:** the composer, after any Text size change in Settings. Both themes, both window sizes.
**What a person sees:** he changes the text size — which is the moment he is looking hardest at the
composer — and the two buttons stand 2 px proud of the field he is about to type in. Exactly the
"otherwise it looks weird" he wrote the instruction about.
**Measured, both directions:**

- 100 % → 110 %: buttons **48 px**, field stays **46 px** (frames 20, 20a).
- 110 % → 100 %: field stays **48 px**, buttons drop to **46 px** (frame 26).
- One character typed → they agree again, at 48 and 48, then 46 and 46 (frames 21, 27).

**Why it is low:** 2 px, transient, self-healing, and only after a settings change. **Why it is not
nothing:** it is visible at 5× (frame 20a), and it breaks his sentence at the one moment he is looking.
**Root cause, offered as a hypothesis for the engineer and not as a finding:** the field's height is set
by an input-driven `autoGrow()`, so a font-size change resizes the CSS-sized buttons but not the field.

### R2 — MEDIUM · The phone does not open the thread at its newest message, so a message that arrives lands unseen

**Where:** the RichOS phone page, on open and on arrival.
**What a person sees:** he opens Rich on his phone and the thread rests roughly one bubble short of the
bottom, so the last exchange is not on screen. Then a message sent from the Mac arrives instantly — and
he never sees it, because the view does not move. He has to scroll by hand to discover it.
**Measured:** at pairing, the last fully visible bubble was `typed-on-mac-candidate-twelve` with the
newest bubble cut off. I then sent `typed-on-mac-candidate-thirteen` from the Mac and filmed the phone
for **15 s across 30 frames: not one contained it** (frame 40 shows it was there all along, below the
fold). After scrolling to the bottom by hand, the very next Mac message was on screen in **0.096 s** and
stayed pinned. So delivery is perfect and the **resting scroll position is wrong**.
**Hypothesis, not a finding:** the `Turn on notifications` banner is ~250 px tall and renders after the
initial scroll-to-bottom, taking the bottom of the thread with it. That banner also holds **~250 px of a
~1325 px page** permanently — with the header it leaves the messages a little over half the screen.

### R3 — LOW-MEDIUM · A message typed on the phone does not appear on the Mac until Rich has finished answering it

**Where:** the Mac window, while the conversation is being driven from the phone.
**What a person sees:** he taps Send on his phone and the Mac thread shows nothing for about six
seconds; then his words and the whole answer appear together. The only sign in between is a small amber
dot on the thread's row in the sidebar.
**Measured:** tap → first appearance on the Mac **5.68 s** (absent at 5.45 s), twice. At **2.99 s** the
thread body was unchanged and only the sidebar row carried a working dot (frame 42); at 5.68 s the
message **and** the finished answer arrived in the same frame (frame 43).
**Why it matters:** the other direction streams — Mac → phone is 0.096 s. The asymmetry is ~60×, and it
is the direction he will use when he is away from his desk.

### R4 — OBSERVATION · The splash composition and the home screen are hard to tell apart

Not a defect and not ranked as one, but it cost this walk two relaunches and nearly produced a false
blocker, so it belongs in the record: the opening screen is a logo card in the middle of a dark field,
while the **home** screen is the full-bleed `round-11/v1` composition. Before I saw a real curtain I had
no way to know which of the two I was looking at, and neither will anyone reading a screenshot. Anything
that judges "is the splash up?" should key on motion or on `#splash`, never on the picture.

## Method notes that keep a false PASS out of the record

### The brief's §62 premise was false, and I nearly filed a blocker on it

The brief said the app would be sitting on the splash. It was sitting on the **home screen** — which
correctly has a settings button under §15 — and I came within one step of reporting "settings button on
a running splash" as a §62 failure. What settled it:

- clicking the **RichOS logo** in the sidebar returns to that same screen, and its memory counter ticks
  (8,494 → 8,537 → 8,541 → 8,592): it is a destination, not a curtain;
- the **Splash screen switch was OFF** for that launch, so no curtain was ever drawn. `splash.js` reads
  that from `localStorage` — the **WebKit store shared with the CEO's real app** — so it does **not**
  come from the copied scratch home, which is why nothing in the scratch `HOME` predicted it.

I switched it on, Rich relaunched, and §62 was then tested for the first time.

### The first trap failed, and the failure would have produced a PASS

Recorded at Rich's instruction, because it is the kind of mistake that reads as success:

- it matched pid `32763`, a `richos-tauri` main process that appeared and vanished, instead of the app
  Rich had launched;
- it filmed a **fixed screen rectangle** `(438,92,1024,700)` rather than the window's own geometry, and
  never checked RichOS was there and in front. It was not. **All 64 frames are of a browser showing the
  `github.com/WebDevBooster/richos` page**;
- so it measured **0.00 % motion** — a static web page — which is the exact signature of a correctly
  held curtain. Had I trusted the number instead of opening the picture, I would have reported §62 PASS
  from frames with no RichOS in them.

The two space presses went to that browser; the frames before and after are pixel-identical, so nothing
scrolled and nothing was activated. The rebuilt trap resolves the process through System Events,
requires it to own a window, brings that process to the front, films the window's **own reported
geometry**, and presses space when the picture **starts moving** rather than at a guessed delay.
