### B1 — the silence safeguard is inert, and I cannot prove this Mac stayed silent

**Basis: ran it, plus `strings` on the published binary.**

`RICHOS_VOICE_LIVE_AUDIO` gates **cargo tests and examples**, not the product:

    $ grep -rn "RICHOS_VOICE_LIVE_AUDIO" --include=*.rs richos/ \
        | grep -vE "build\.rs|examples/|ignore = |//!|///|// "
    richos/app/crates/richos-voice/src/lib.rs:108:  std::env::var("RICHOS_VOICE_LIVE_AUDIO").as_deref() == Ok("1"),
    richos/app/crates/richos-voice/src/lib.rs:111:  `--ignored`. Re-run as `RICHOS_VOICE_LIVE_AUDIO=1 cargo test -p richos-voice`, \

Both surviving hits are the test-harness guard itself. And decisively:

    $ strings -a .../RichOS.app/Contents/MacOS/richos-tauri | grep -c RICHOS_VOICE_LIVE_AUDIO
    0

**The literal is absent from the shipped binary**, and a Rust `std::env::var("…")` call embeds its
key as a string literal — so the inference, stated as an inference, is that **the published nightly
never consults that variable**. Setting it or not setting it changed nothing I could observe.
Meanwhile the voice turn configured a real output path and said so:

    [richos-voice] in=injected wav (…/probe2.wav) 16000 Hz/1 ch · out=External Headphones 48000 Hz/2 ch
                   · stt=small.en · tts=macOS say · Daniel · 180 wpm · aec=PBFDAF 2048 taps …

and the UI displayed, on screen, with a stop control:

> ● **Rich is speaking**   [tap to stop]

**So: a voice turn in this app speaks by default, through whatever output device is selected, and
nothing in the brief's method prevented it.** Output volume was read before and after every step and
never changed (`25, muted:false` throughout) — but volume is not evidence of silence, and `say`
would not change it. The output device was **External Headphones**, so anything emitted went there
rather than to room speakers, which is probably why nothing was heard. **I cannot prove this Mac
stayed silent, and I am not going to claim it did.**

Compounding it: **there is no in-app control to silence Rich.** A sweep of `ui/main.js` for a mute
or speak-aloud setting returns only microphone and silence-detection code (positive control: `grep -c
voice` on the same file returns 151). Leaving voice mode is the only way to stop him speaking.

**What would settle whether sound played:** a boot with an audio-capture loopback device selected as
output, or a `tts`/`playout` debug line recording bytes actually written to the device. Neither
exists today. **What the next walk needs before voice is in scope again:** a real runtime gate
(`RICHOS_TTS_SINK=null` or equivalent) that the shipped binary respects — the variable the brief
named does not, and quoting it in a future brief will produce the same false comfort.

## One thing I got wrong, recorded because it touched his machine

Early on I clicked by screen coordinate without re-focusing the app in the same step. The RichOS
window had lost focus to Chrome, and **the click landed in his browser** — System Events reported it
hit `UI element "RichOS — connected" … of application process Google Chrome`. A page element, almost
certainly harmless, but it was his browser and not mine.

Two frames captured in that window also contained Chrome content rather than RichOS. **Both were
deleted immediately and neither is committed.**

Fixed for the rest of the run, and the fix is the durable part: **every click after that point went
through the accessibility tree by control name** (`click button "Download my speech model" …`), which
cannot land in another application, and the screenshot helper gained the frontmost check described
above. Anyone repeating this walk should start with both.

### 1. The first screen, as he will see it — `01-first-screen.png`

**Basis: ran it on screen.**

The splash is genuinely beautiful and the copy is plain, American, and free of jargon. Every string
reads like a person wrote it.

**Every number on it is invented** — `LORO · 14 MONTHS · 7,509 MEMORIES`, `18 AI SPECIALISTS ·
6 working now`, `1,596 TASKS HANDLED WITHOUT YOU`, `4,801 SOURCES UNDERSTOOD`, `430 DECISIONS
REMEMBERED`, `2,365 h OF YOUR ATTENTION SAVED`, and a `WORKING NOW` list of six named specialists
with live-looking status dots — on an install whose own boot log says `0 compan(ies)`, `central
folder … (NOT THERE)`, `no corpus configured`.

**It is labeled, and the label is honest and well-placed:**

> This is what your home screen could look like once Rich knows enough about you and your business.

**I am not filing this as a defect**, and I want to be explicit about why, because it is a close
call. The disclaimer is present, it is the *second* thing the eye lands on after the artwork, and it
measures **8.92:1** — better contrast than several things that are real. The one element I would
still question is `WORKING NOW`, which with its live dots reads as present-tense status rather than
illustration; everything else is framed as a preview. **That is a judgment for Urban, not a QA
failure, and I am flagging it rather than ruling on it.**

Two smaller notes: the splash appears on **every** launch, not just the first (there is an "Opening
screen" toggle in Settings, so this is a preference, not a defect); and the category labels drawn
*over* the artwork (`866` under REVENUE, `1,318` under CUSTOMERS) are the hardest text on the screen
to read — light glyphs with a dark outline over a bright nebula. See the contrast note for why I did
not put a number on those.

### 2. The model download, as he will see it — **the best thing in this release**

**Basis: ran it on screen, twice, plus a negative control.**

Pressing the talk control on a model-missing machine shows exactly what the previous audit predicted
from source — now **verified on screen** rather than hypothesized:

> I can hear you once I download my speech model. It's 487.6 MB, and it's a one-time download.
> [Download my speech model]

The progress, sampled from the live control every 1.2 s:

    t+0s   Downloading my speech model — 0% of 487.6 MB.
    t+3s   … 11% …        t+12s  … 44% …        t+20s  … 74% …
    t+25s  … 93% …        t+27s  … 98% …
    t+28s  Checking my speech model is the one I expected…
    t+29s  My ears are installed. Start listening whenever you're ready.

**29 seconds for 487.6 MB.** Monotonic, no stalls, the size repeated throughout so he always knows
what it is costing him, **the integrity step shown as its own honest state**, and a completion
sentence in plain English. (One sample read `missing value` at t+22s — an accessibility-read blip
between repaints, not a gap in the UI.)

**The bytes are right, and the app proves it itself:**

    $ shasum -a 256 …/home2/.config/richos/models/ggml-small.en.bin
    c6138d6d58ecc8322097e0f987c32f1be8bb0a18532a3f88f734d1bbf9c41e5d   (= the pin at ecf8cdaf)
    $ ls -la … → 487614201 bytes                                       (= the pinned byte count)

    [richos] voice model: ggml-small.en.bin installed and verified against its pinned sha256
             (487614201 bytes, resumed from 0)

Reproduced on two independent downloads, identical both times.

**The negative control — the case the previous audit could not reach.** I patched 17 bytes into the
middle of the installed model, leaving the size byte-identical (`487614201`) and the hash different —
precisely the substitution a size check would wave through. On the next launch:

    richos-voice: REFUSING TO LISTEN: the model file for "small.en" is not the model RichOS pinned.
      Expected sha256 c6138d6d58ec…, found 1e0eecb3e33b… at …/ggml-small.en.bin. These are different
      weights under the right name — a different model would hear different words and nothing
      downstream would know. Re-fetch the model (`richos-service fetch-model small.en`) …

**That is the single best-written line in the product.** It names both hashes, the path, the actual
risk, and the fix. The refusal is correct, and it is exactly the failure that would otherwise be
invisible. **Its user-facing half is D3.**

### 3. A silent voice turn — transcript yes, answer no

**Basis: ran it on screen.** Input: `say -o probe2.wav --data-format=LEI16@16000` (WAVE, 1 ch,
16000 Hz, 2.348 s, 75124 bytes); volume identical before and after.

- **The microphone was never opened, and the app says so in its own words** —
  `[richos-voice] INJECTED INPUT: …/probe2.wav (16000 Hz, 1 ch, 2.348 s) — this is NOT the microphone`.
  The hot-mic invariant held. Positive control: `grep -c richos` on the same log returns 26 lines,
  so the log was being written and read.
- **Pressing talk on a model-missing machine did not open any stream.** The log stayed at 26 lines
  across the press — the UI entered a voice-pending state and offered the download instead.
- **The transcript rendered, correctly and promptly** — "What did I decide about nightly releases?"
  appeared as a user bubble (`11-listening.png`). STT works end to end from a fresh install.
- **The answer never arrived.** The turn failed — see D2.
- **The audio question the brief asked is answered by B1, and the answer is none of the three
  options offered:** it is not silently dropped, not shown as text, and not merely *claimed* —
  **the "live audio off" state the brief assumed was never in effect.** The UI said "Rich is
  speaking" and a real output device was configured, and I found no runtime switch in the shipped
  app that would have prevented it. Whether bytes actually reached the headphones is the one thing
  I could not determine; see B1 for what would settle it.

### 4. A text turn, then quit and relaunch

**Basis: ran it on screen.**

- **The text turn failed identically to the voice turn** (`14-text-sent.png`) — same "Stopped", same

- **Thread persistence: PASS, and cleanly** (`24-relaunch-thread.png`). After a clean ⌘Q and

### 5. Anything wrong for a person — the defects

#### D1 — Setup completes, and the next launch asks him to do it again (High)

**Basis: ran it on screen + disk + log. Reproduced on four consecutive launches.**

I ran setup to completion. The app said so itself:

> That's the setting up done. The software is installed.

The engine is genuinely on disk, written at that moment:

    $ ls "…/home2/Library/Application Support/RichOS/engine" | wc -l    → 44 entries, created 08:36

**On the very next launch, the log says:**

    [richos] engine directory: /Users/alex/.claude/richos-engine (via engine install pointer)
    [richos] first-run setup: the RichOS engine is NOT installed — looked in:

**Note the end of that line: it names ZERO paths.** The "looked in" list is empty. A detector with no
candidate paths can never find anything, which is consistent with it always reporting "NOT installed"
— and the sheet duly re-appears (`24-relaunch-thread.png`, `26-model-refused.png`). The corpus sheet
("Where should I keep what you tell me?") re-asks on every launch too.

For him that means: **do the setup, and be asked to do it again every single time he opens the app.**
That is the kind of thing that makes a person stop trusting what the app tells them.

**What I did not prove, stated plainly:** I closed the "Connect account" step rather than drive an
OAuth flow against his real Anthropic account in his browser. So I cannot say whether connecting the
account clears this sheet. What I *can* say is that the sheet's own subject — the RichOS engine — is
installed, and the line that reports on it names no search paths at all. **The empty `looked in:`
list is a defect on its own terms regardless of how the account question resolves.**

#### D2 — A failed turn hides the cause the app is holding (High)

**Basis: ran it on screen + the app's own conversation ledger.**

On screen, twice (`14-text-sent.png`):

> **Stopped after 1s**
> I hit a snag mid-thought and had to stop — say the word and I'll pick it back up.
> Everything I'd already written above is saved.
> [Pick it back up]

In `conversation-ledger.jsonl`, at the same instant:

    {"event":"TurnInterrupted","turn_id":"turn_afe3ad4a…",
     "reason":"cognition protocol: \"Not logged in · Please run /login\"", …}

**The app has the exact, actionable, correctly-worded cause and shows him something else.** Four
separate problems, in descending order of harm:

1. **It blames a transient hiccup for a permanent condition.** "I hit a snag mid-thought" invites a
   retry. No number of retries will log anyone in.
2. **"Everything I'd already written above is saved" is not true here.** Nothing was written. There
   is no partial answer above it. It reassures about something that does not exist.
3. **It never mentions the account** — even though the app offered to connect one on first run, knows
   it was declined, and has a working route to it (Settings → Account connection).
4. **The status "Stopped" is where the real information should be** and carries none.

**Why this matters beyond my harness:** it is fair to say I caused this particular instance by not
connecting an account. But **"not logged in" is not an exotic state — it is where every expiring
subscription session lands.** When his token expires mid-morning, this is the screen he gets, and it
will tell him Rich hit a snag and invite him to press a button that cannot work.

#### D3 — When the model fails its integrity check, voice disappears without a word (Medium)

**Basis: ran it on screen + accessibility tree, with a positive control.**

With the corrupted model in place, the boot log refuses beautifully (quoted in item 2). The user-facing
half of the same event:

- The user-facing sentence is **only in the log**: *"Something about my hearing changed on this
  machine and I'd rather not guess at what you said than get it wrong."*
- **On screen, the talk control is simply gone** (`26-model-refused.png`). Not disabled with a
  reason — absent.

Confirmed structurally rather than from pixels:

    $ … 'entire contents of window "RichOS"' | grep -iE 'checkbox|speech|listening|download|hearing'
    (NONE — no talk control, no voice text, no download offer)
    $ … | grep -c "of window RichOS"          # positive control: the tree IS readable
    111

So in the one case that actually matters for correctness — **the model has been swapped for different
weights under the right name** — he gets no message, no explanation, and no way to re-download. Voice
just stops existing. Compare the `model-missing` state, which handles this well: button present,
plain sentence, one-press fix. **The `refused` state should do the same thing, and it is a smaller
change than it sounds.**

#### D4 — A failed text turn is invisible to the operator log (Medium)

The failed **voice** turn logged `[richos] voice turn failed: cognition io: …`. The failed **text**
turn logged **nothing at all** — the log sat at 44 lines across it. The `gui-boot` log is the one
artifact meant to hold every line of a launch to account, and the most common failure path writes
nothing to it.

#### D5 — First run is four interruptions deep before he can type (Low–Medium)

In order, on a clean install: **(1)** the splash; **(2)** "There's one thing I need on this Mac"
(engine) — dismissible; **(3)** "Where should I keep what you tell me?" (corpus) — dismissible;
**(4)** "Which company is this copy of Rich for?" — **not dismissible, no "Not now"**; then **(5)** an
in-thread offer of a ~20-minute interview. Only after all of it does he reach an input box.

Sheets 2 and 3 offer "Not now"; sheet 4 does not, which is at least inconsistent and at most a wall.
The interview offer (5) has the best copy of the set — *"'Not now' means I'll stop offering. You can
start it any time by asking."* — it says what the choice costs. Worth copying to the others.

#### D6 — "Pick it back up" hands the work back to him (Low)

The message says *"say the word and I'll pick it back up"*; the button says "Pick it back up". Pressing
it **puts his text back in the composer** (`15-after-retry.png`) — no new turn, no log line. Returning
the text is useful behavior; the words promise that Rich will resume, and he does not.

#### D7 — Unstyled native controls and truncated text (Low, cosmetic)

- The **Account type** dropdown (`19-setup-progress.png`) and the **Company** dropdown
  (`16-settings.png`) render as plain white macOS `<select>`s with blue arrows, against both the dark
  and the cream theme. They are the only unstyled objects in an otherwise very carefully finished UI.
- **"Home screen" button label is truncated to "Company butt…"** in Settings.
- Long paths and URLs **hard-wrap mid-word** (`…/githubus` / `ercontent.com`; `…Ric` / `hOS/corpus`).
  His own paths are short, so this mostly bites operators — but the update server URL is shown to
  everyone.

#### D8 — The talk control has no accessible name (Low, accessibility)

The accessibility tree is, on the whole, **unusually good** — `Search ⌘K`, `+ New thread`,
`New thread in Acme Coaching`, `Actions for Running`, `Light theme`, `Follow the system`,
`Dark theme`, and even `No name is set. Open preferences to add yours.` are all properly named, and
that is better than most shipping apps.

Two controls are not: the **talk control** is exposed as bare `checkbox 1`, and one sidebar control as
bare `button 1`. VoiceOver announces the primary affordance of a voice-first product as "checkbox".
`pop up button ⚙` is named only by its glyph.

- **Contrast: PASS, in BOTH themes, at all 41 nodes measured in the state where the text is meant to

- **Provisioning end to end**: 29 s, honest progress, explicit integrity step, hash matching the pin

- **The integrity refusal**: correct, and the best-written message in the product.
- **Thread persistence** across a clean quit, with both turns intact and theme preserved.
- **Clean vs unclean shutdown told apart correctly** — `fresh` here, `crash-restart` in the prior

- **The updater reports itself current on screen** — `RichOS 1.2.0-nightly.20260917.1 is up to date`

- **The microphone was never opened**, and the app said so in its own log rather than leaving it to
  be inferred.
- **Setup is recoverable**: after declining it, Settings → Account connection brings the sheet back.
  He is not stranded — though nothing in the error message points him there (D2).

### The contrast measurements

Method, declared because it decides what the numbers mean: each node is sampled from the committed
PNG; the background is the modal color of the rect and the foreground the mean of the pixels furthest
from it in luminance (the glyph core). Ratios are sRGB relative luminance per WCAG 2.1.

**Two states were excluded and why.** *(a)* Anything **behind an open modal sheet** — the app dims
background chrome to roughly 1.8:1 while a sheet is up. I measured that, nearly filed it as a
failure, and then ran the control: in the clean state the same sidebar measures **6.4–6.6:1**. The
dimming is deliberate de-emphasis of content that is not the focus, and **I am declaring it exempt
rather than passing it silently.** *(b)* The **category labels over the splash artwork** — light
glyphs with a dark outline over a bright animated nebula. No automated worst-case measure I tried was
trustworthy there (one called a plainly white-on-dark button 1.16:1 by picking a stray pixel), so
**I am declaring these unmeasured rather than publishing a number I do not believe.** By eye they are
the weakest text in the product; a designer should look at `866` and `1,318` specifically.
