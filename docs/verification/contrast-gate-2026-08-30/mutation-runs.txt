THE CONTRAST GATE — THE TWENTY-TWO RUNS THAT PROVE THE SUITE CAN FAIL
=====================================================================

`app/ui/tests/contrast.js` reports 24 checks green over 15 declared, across ten surfaces
in two themes. That number is worth nothing on its own. A checker that walks a DOM and
finds nothing is indistinguishable, from the outside, from a checker that walks nothing —
and this repository has produced both kinds of green in one week: a scanner reporting CLEAN
over an empty corpus, and a `run.js` reporting "all 4 suites passed" over a suite it was
not running.

It matters more here than usual, because of what this gate is FOR. The CEO's order was
"I don't want to repeat myself again or remind anyone that the contrast basics must be
maintained." The answer to that order cannot be another sentence in another prompt. If this
suite is green and wrong, the order has been answered with a lie that is harder to detect
than the original silence.

So every check was run RED, once, by breaking the thing it guards — mostly in the SHIPPED
source (`app/ui/index.html`, `app/ui/style.css`), and where the check guards the gate's own
arithmetic, in `app/ui/tests/lib/contrast.js`.

The driver applied exactly one edit per run, refused to proceed unless the target string
matched exactly, restored the file from a copy taken before the edit whether the run passed
or failed, and `git status` was clean at the end. It is `scratchpad/mutate.py` from the
session that produced this file; the mutations are also listed inside `contrast.js` itself,
against their check numbers, so the two cannot drift silently.


TWO RUNS DID NOT GO RED, AND ARE RECORDED AS RUNS THAT DID NOT GO RED
--------------------------------------------------------------------

  * MUTATION 11, FIRST ATTEMPT, WAS INERT. `stackFor`'s modal branch was changed from
    `if (modal && !modal.contains(el))` to `if (modal)`, on the theory that a dialog's own
    contents would then be filed as obscured behind the dialog they are inside, be measured
    nowhere, and be caught as orphans. Nothing went red. The loop that line sits in only
    iterates elements painted ABOVE the node, and a desk card inside the panel has none, so
    the mutated line was never reached on any surface. It proves nothing about check 11.

    Redone as a curtain: a `pointer-events: none`, `rgba(0,0,0,0.6)`, z-index-300 div left
    over the whole app in `index.html`. That is blunt — it reds fifteen checks — and it is
    the honest shape of the defect: the `obscured` bucket only becomes a hiding place when
    something covers everything, and check 11 is what refuses to let a covered app read as
    a clean one.

  * MUTATION 13b, FIRST ATTEMPT, MISSED ITS ANCHOR. `.desk-card-target {` does not appear
    in `style.css`; the rule is written `.desk-card-target,` at the head of a selector list.
    The driver refused to proceed and recorded ANCHOR-MISSING rather than editing something
    near it. That refusal is the reason an anchor miss here is a recorded non-run and not a
    silent pass. Redone against the real anchor, it went red.


ONE MUTATION IS WORTH SINGLING OUT
----------------------------------

MUTATION 10 is the only one that needed two simultaneous edits, and it is the one that
matters most.

Check 10 exists because the app ships ONE theme. There is no `prefers-color-scheme: dark`
rule in `style.css` or `splash.css`, so the "dark" half of every walk is a same-palette
re-check under a dark OS preference — not dark-mode coverage. Reporting that honestly is
the easy branch, and it is not the branch worth proving.

The branch worth proving is the other one: the day a dark theme lands, does this suite
actually exercise it, or does it keep reporting green over a light palette while claiming
both themes? So the mutation adds a real dark block to `style.css` AND removes
`colorScheme` from the suite's own `newPage` call. The suite says:

    1 `prefers-color-scheme: dark` block(s) are shipped, but the dark run produced the
    identical palette — the emulation is not reaching them, so the dark half of every check
    above is a fiction

That is the sentence that has to exist for "both themes" to mean anything later.
