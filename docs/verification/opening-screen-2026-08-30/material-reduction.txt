THE THREE THINGS THAT COULD NOT COME ACROSS AS THEY ARE
=======================================================

Named first, because they are the whole cost of this round and burying them in a per-version
table would be the wrong shape for a reader.

1. GENERATED GEOMETRY. Three studies build shapes in JavaScript against the mat's MEASURED
   box: v7's saddle stitching (196 stitches, seeded jitter), v14's blind-debossed frame (one
   stroked, filtered rect), v18's planished facet field (~270 shaded quads). A JSON library
   cannot hold a generator, and `tests/splash.js` check 1 exists to keep it that way.

     v14 needed no generator at all — the study's SVG rect is a bordered div once you
     account for an SVG stroke straddling its path where a CSS border sits inside its box.
     v7's stitching was RUN ONCE and its output baked (see below).
     v18's could not be, and v18 is not here.

2. POINTER TRACKING. v10, v13, v15 and v16 move a specular with the pointer; v17 warms its
   picture light as the pointer approaches; v12 parallaxes a glow; v7 lets you brush the nap.
   NOBODY MOVES A POINTER OVER A SPLASH SCREEN. It is up for about a quarter of a second and
   it is `pointer-events: none` for all of it. So each of these is pinned at its rest
   position, which is what `splash.css` already does to the lamp for the same reason.

     WHERE "REST" IS, derived rather than eyeballed. The splash's lamp is drawn at 40%/40%
     of the VIEWPORT and the plinth is centred, so the lamp's position ON THE MAT is
     (0.40 x W - left) / width. Measured under WebKit:

       1280 x 800   plinth 601.56 x 496.30 at (339.22, 151.84)  ->  28.7% / 33.9%
       1680 x 1050  plinth 688.00 x 581.75 at (496.00, 234.13)  ->  25.6% / 32.0%
       1024 x 700   plinth 481.25 x 431.59 at (271.38, 134.20)  ->  28.7% / 33.8%

     Written as `29% 34%`. The studies open their speculars at a DIFFERENT point — 59.6% /
     33.9% at 1280x800 — for one reason only: their mat is pushed left by the 348px palette
     rail, so the same viewport-relative lamp lands somewhere else on it. Reproducing the
     study's number would have put the highlight where the shipping lamp is not, which is
     worse than not moving it.

3. A GENERATOR'S OUTPUT IS DATA; A GENERATOR IS NOT. v7's stitching was produced by running
   the study's OWN generator once, verbatim — same `mulberry32(41)`, same 10.5px pitch, same
   7px corner radius, same 14px inset, same per-stitch jitter of length, angle and position —
   at the mat's measured size (601.5625 x 496.296875 at 1280x800, rounded to 602 x 496), and
   the 196 stitches it produced are what the entry carries. It scales with the mat under
   `background-size: 100% 100%`, exactly as the logo and the wordmark already do under
   `clamp()`.

     Cost: 20,262 B as three separate stroked paths. Written once into a `<defs>` and
     `<use>`d three times at three offsets — the dark under-thread, the gold, and the light
     on its top edge — it is 5,064 B. The needle holes (398 dots at r=1.05, drawn UNDER a
     2.5px under-thread that covers them) were dropped and are the difference between those
     two figures plus 6.4 KB.

v17  OIL AND GOLD LEAF, UNDER A PICTURE LIGHT                        ADDED (token set widened)

     existing tokens. The gold-leaf frame is the plinth's `surfaceImage` plus its inset
     shadow rings; the frame's rabbet is `keylineShadow` with `keylineWidth: 0px`; the canvas
     is one layer with three background images and a `background-blend-mode` list.
     Differs in ONE structural way, named: THE PICTURE LIGHT is an element with two
     pseudo-elements in the study (bar, stem, spill) and three layers here, because an entry

     on a mat that resizes: the bar `-17px 32% auto 32%` x 8px; the stem
     `-25px calc(50% - 13px) auto calc(50% - 13px)` x 8px; the spill at 8% of the bar's own

     centre — `hypot(640-512, 400-320)` at 1280x800 — and the study's own formula

     and nothing was chosen by eye.

v18  PLANISHED BRONZE, MIDNIGHT PATINA                               *** LEFT OUT ***
     ITS MATERIAL IS A LIGHTING SOLVE, NOT A TEXTURE. The study builds ~270 jittered quads
     over the mat and shades each one against a light position:

         nz = sqrt(1 - nx^2 - ny^2)
         b  = .24 + 1.02 * max(0, (nx*dx + ny*dy + nz*dz) / |d|)

     per facet, with a per-facet tone offset and a three-segment tone ramp that turns warm
     above b = .6. There is no CSS or SVG primitive for a per-facet normal.

     THE TWO WAYS IT COULD HAVE BEEN FAKED, and why neither was taken:
       * BAKE THE FIELD as a polygon SVG, the way v7's stitching was baked. v7's stitching is
         a ONE-DIMENSIONAL run along an edge and survives scaling; a facet field is a
         two-dimensional lighting solution computed for ONE plinth size, and the plinth is
         `clamp()`-sized (481px wide at 1024x700, 688px at 1680x1050). The baked speculars
         would keep pointing at a light that is no longer where they were solved for, and it
         would cost roughly 33 KB in a 60 KB library, parsed on every launch including the
         seventeen launches that draw something else.
       * DROP THE FACETS and keep the rest. What remains is a flat #0D1526 mat with a 2px
         dotted gold keyline and a chased mark. That is v0 with a different name, and
         shipping it as v18 would be a lie about what the CEO chose.

     A MISSING VARIATION IS BETTER THAN A LYING ONE. If v18 is wanted, the honest route is a
     WebGL or canvas layer in the renderer, which is a different decision from this round's —
     it puts drawing code back into `splash.js` and the whole contract that check 1 and
     check 2 defend exists to keep it out.


WHAT THE WIDENING ACTUALLY ADDED, AND WHY IT IS STILL DATA
==========================================================

  surfaceImage    the mat's paint when it is a gradient (v8, v10, v13, v15, v16, v17)
  keylineWidth    the inner keyline's width, style, border-image and shadow — enough for a
  keylineStyle      gold thread (v8-v11, v13, v15), a chevron or vertical gold border-image
  keylineImage      (v12, v16), a dotted chase, the rabbet shadow of a picture frame (v17),
  keylineShadow     or its removal (v7, v14)
  materials       an ordered stack of flat layers over the mat, each one a `background`, a
                  blend mode, an opacity, an inset, a radius, a border, a mask
  markFilter      a CSS filter string the entry writes out (unused today)
  signalFilter    the same, on the gold alone (v13, v16)
  relief          an SVG filter described as VALUES

`relief` is the one that deserves the argument. `splash.js` fixes its SHAPE — a noise field
clipped inside the mark, then any number of bands, then a merge order of
`outer bands -> SourceGraphic -> noise -> inner bands` — and the entry supplies every number.
That is the SAME division the gilding gradient has lived under since the first seven entries:
`gilding()` writes the offsets and the vector, the library writes the stops.

The order is deliberately NOT data. Any other order draws a shadow on top of the thing
casting it, and check 16 asserts it against the entry's own band placements.

The guards are unchanged and still hold:
  check 1  JSON-parses this library, so it cannot contain a function
  check 2  greps splash.js and splash.css for a colour literal, and finds none
  check 14 refuses an entry whose material stack carries a key the renderer does not know
