# The phone path on nightly `.7`, walked end to end inside the test VM

**Verdict: NOT READY FOR HIM.**

Every defect this nightly set out to fix is fixed, and I could not find one of them still on the
screen. The path fails on something nobody had measured before, because nobody had ever got this far:
**a sentence typed on the phone takes 30–55 seconds to reach the Mac**, and one message in four was
refused outright. R3 — the claim under test — is *not* the cause: once a message arrives, the open
thread draws it in **320 ms**.

| Step | | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One path, `This Mac is ready`, one press to the code, three controls in the window, reopened sheet focuses a control | **PASS** |
| 2 | Settings says a code is live and for how long | **PASS** |
| 3 | Paired from Safari in the guest; the six words matched; both sides showed paired | **PASS** |
| 4 | Phone → Mac | **FAIL** — arrival took 48.8 s (also 30.7 s, 54.5 s; one send refused). The repaint after arrival was 320 ms. |
| 5 | Mac → phone | **NOT REACHED** — the guest has no engine, so the Mac refuses desk messages before they become a bubble |
| 6 | `Stop and go back` → `This Mac is ready`; port 8443 refuses from the host | **PASS** |

**Verification mode.** macOS 15.7.7 guest in the Tart test VM (`--vm ray7`), app window **1024×700
at (438,92)**, one app window, Safari in the same guest as the phone, side by side and not
overlapping. Two-user test: yes — the Mac and the phone page are the two ends. Nothing was rendered,
installed or launched on the host; the host's `pgrep -x richos-tauri` is empty and was never mine.
Every claim below is **verified live on the guest's screen** unless it says otherwise. Contrast
ratios are computed from rendered pixels, never eyeballed.

**Freshness.** Bundle `RichOS-1.2.0-nightly.20260919.7-macos-aarch64.zip` carries
`RichOSSourceCommit = a5557b34a5d3f47fae2d2d015909239904301feb` in its own `Info.plist`.
`a5557b34` is `321f1076` plus a version bump only (`git diff --stat 321f1076 a5557b34 -- richos/app/`
= `nightly-build.json`, `Cargo.lock`, `Cargo.toml`, `tauri.conf.json`). The running app agreed on
screen: Settings → Updates read **"RichOS 1.2.0-nightly.20260919.7 is up to date."**

## Defects, ranked

### 1. BLOCKING — a sentence typed on the phone takes 30–55 seconds to reach the Mac

This is the finding. It is not R3, and it is not the rendering.

The Mac's own intake log dates the arrival, so the trip splits cleanly in two:

| Send | leg 1 — send → Mac intake | leg 2 — intake → repaint |
|---|---|---|
| `ray r3 seven check` | **30.67 s** | not sampled (capture ended first) |
| `ray r3 pass two` | **54.47 s** | not sampled (capture ended first) |
| `ray r3 final probe` | **48.79 s** | **320 ms** |
| `ray r3 pass three` | **never arrived** — the phone showed `Not sent.` and `One message could not be sent. Your Mac did not accept it.` | — |

What the user sees: they type, they press Send, the phone says **Delivered**, and the Mac shows
nothing for the better part of a minute. On a phone, away from the desk, that reads as "it did not
work" long before it works. One message in four never arrived at all.

**A lead, not a conclusion.** `richos/web/web-app/lib/link.js:66` sets `MAX_RETRY_MS = 30000`, and
every measured delay is ≥30 s with the spread of a backoff rather than a fixed cost. That fits a
first attempt failing and the message going out only on a retry. Why the first attempt fails is
open. The server is not a single-threaded queue — `listen.rs:388` spawns per connection — so that is
not it.

**Caveat, stated because it could change the reading.** This guest has no RichOS engine, so every
phone message starts a turn that fails, and desk messages are refused outright. I cannot rule out
that the engine's absence is what makes the first attempt fail. It needs one re-run in a guest with
`claude` signed in — the one-time human step named in `docs/testvm.md`. **It should not be assumed
to be an artifact**: the phone's own retry ceiling is 30 s, the delays are ≥30 s, and nothing about
the missing engine obviously explains a failed *delivery* as opposed to a failed *reply*.

### 2. HIGH — the Mac says "It is paired" before the user confirms the six words

On the pairing screen the phone asks the user to check six words and offers `They match — pair this
phone` / `They do not match`. At that moment the Mac's sheet **already** read `Phone — It is paired.
Open Rich on it and keep talking.` Both screens were on screen together (frame 13).

The six words exist so a person can detect that something other than their Mac answered. A user
looking at both screens sees the Mac assert the thing the phone is still asking them to verify, which
teaches them the check is ceremonial. Whatever the crypto is doing underneath, the wording should not
settle the question before the person has answered it.

### 3. MEDIUM — the pairing can be stopped by a macOS keychain modal, and the app cannot see it

Pressing `Set my phone up` raised a **`SecurityAgent`** modal — *"Keychain Not Found — A keychain
cannot be found to store 'certificate-authority-key.'"* — from the app's own shell-out in
`phone/secrets.rs` (`/usr/bin/security`, `CA_KEY` at line 50). The sheet sat on `Getting this Mac
ready...` behind it.

**The app's recovery is genuinely good** and deserves saying: on Cancel it returned
*"Your Mac's keychain would not let me keep the key your phone needs. Nothing was changed, and I have
not stored anything."* — plain, honest, and it re-enabled the button.

The risk is narrow but real: `security` is a GUI-capable tool, and the app has no timeout or guard
around a call that can block on a dialog. `secrets.rs`'s written doctrine covers the `-w` argument
exposure; it does not mention that the tool can draw a window. On the CEO's Mac the login keychain
exists and is unlocked at login, so he is unlikely to meet this one — but a locked keychain would
park the pairing behind a modal with no app-side recovery.

**In this walk the trigger was the fixture, not the product** — see defect 4.

### 4. MEDIUM (harness) — the QA fixture home symlinks the CEO's real keychain

`<fixture-home>/Library/Keychains` is a **symlink to `/Users/alex/Library/Keychains`** — the host
user's own keychain directory. It is the only host-pointing symlink in the 1.8 GB fixture
(`find . -type l -lname '/Users/alex*'` → exactly one hit).

In the guest it dangles, which is what raised defect 3. On the **host**, a run with `HOME` pointed at
this fixture would have `/usr/bin/security` read and write the CEO's **real login keychain** under
service `com.richos.app.phone-channel` — precisely what `secrets.rs` says must never happen:
*"the tests must not write to the CEO's real Keychain. A test that leaves a key behind in his login
keychain is garbage by CEO decision §54, and a test that deletes one is worse."*

I replaced it inside my disposable guest with a real keychain to finish the walk. The fixture on disk
still has the symlink; it should be a real directory, or absent.

### 5. LOW (harness) — the tailnet join loses a race with `tailscaled` and degrades silently

`run.sh` attempted the join **11 s** after the guest reported an IP and got
*"failed to connect to local tailscaled ... /var/run/tailscaled.socket: no such file or directory"*.
It printed `tailnet=not-joined` and carried on. A later `tailnet.sh join ray7` succeeded in 38 s with
a certificate.

Without noticing that line a tester would walk the whole phone path against a Mac that cannot serve,
read *"This Mac is not set up yet"*, and file it as a product defect. The join should wait for the
daemon's socket rather than for a fixed interval.

### 6. LOW — small things worth one line each

* **The theme selector is three checkboxes, not radios.** AX reports `checkbox Light theme` for three
  mutually exclusive options; a screen-reader user is told each can be independently on.
* **Two gears, two different panels.** The bottom-left `⚙` opens Preferences; the top-right one opens
  Settings. Only the top-right exposes an accessible name (`Settings`); the bottom-left's AX title is
  the glyph `⚙`.
* **`Paired with Phone` is a near-empty sentence** when the device reports a generic name. The code
  imagines `Paired with Pixel 8`; what rendered was `Paired with Phone`.
* **Three "Not now" offers stack up at launch** before the home screen is usable (engine, memory
  folder, questions). Recorded as **carried, not new** — offer-sheet work landed on main as
  `8ed50863` *after* this nightly, and is not in `.7`.

### Step 4 in detail, and the two false starts

My first two measurements found **no change at all** in the thread pane for 14.7 s and 37 s and I
was about to report R3 as still broken. Both were wrong, and the instrument was the reason: the
message had not arrived yet. The intake log settled it. The lesson is the one that already has a
name here — a negative result needs a positive probe before it is believed.

The desk positive control I reached for **does not work in this guest**: a desk message is refused
outright (*"I can't take that on yet — the RichOS engine isn't on this Mac"*), so it never becomes a
bubble. That is also why step 5 is NOT REACHED rather than failed.

### Sampling resolution

`screencapture` in the guest costs ~87 ms for a small region and ~319 ms for the whole window, so the
brief's ~45 ms interval is not reachable with it. The R3 number therefore carries roughly ±170 ms
(the median inter-frame gap on the deciding run). That is far finer than the 320 ms it measured and
the 7,288 ms it was compared against.

## What would change the verdict

One re-run of step 4 in a guest with `claude` signed in. If a sentence typed on the phone lands on
the Mac in a second or two there, defect 1 is an artifact of a guest with no engine and this path is
ready but for defect 2. If it still takes half a minute, defect 1 is the phone path's headline
problem and no amount of correct rendering makes the feature usable.
