**Defect 2.1 — the first message after the refresh still pays the full prime.** *Verified on screen and

## 3 — The Tailscale screens on the Mac. FAIL

*Verification basis: verified on screen, every string read off the pixels.*

### What is right, and it is a lot

- **The how-to screens were correctly passed over.** This Mac is signed in with certificates enabled, so
  the flow went straight from the chooser to "This Mac is ready" with the tailnet name — exactly as it
  should.
- **The pairing address is the tailnet name on 8443**, as expected:
  `https://mm1.tail770f6e.ts.net:8443/#pair=XXXXXXXX`.
- **§61.1, the identity trap, is genuinely crystal-clear.** The screen names the provider, names the
  identity, says "exactly what this Mac used", and says what goes wrong if he gets it wrong: *"A
  different provider makes a different network, and then the two will never see each other."* It also
  says *"There is no pairing step in Tailscale. Sign in with the same account on both devices and they
  are connected."* That is the bar the CEO set, and this clears it.
- **It detects his phone**: "Your phone (HONOR X6b) is on this network."
- **"There is no certificate to install on this path. If your phone asks you to install a profile,
  something is wrong — tell me."** Correct, and it matched reality (see §4).

### Defect 3.1 — the lead paragraph contradicts the option he just chose. *Verified on screen.*

Every screen in this flow opens with:

> "Your phone talks to this Mac directly, **over your own home network**. Nothing of what you say goes anywhere else, and there is no account to make."

That describes the **"At home only"** option. It stays on screen unchanged after he picks **"Anywhere,
including away from the office"** — which is the Tailscale path, is not the home network, and **does**
involve making an account. §61 exists because he wants this usable away from home; the standing first
sentence tells him the opposite. Screenshots 13, 14, 15, 17.

*Related, smaller:* the same flow calls the place "the office", "the house" and "home" in three
adjacent sentences.

### Defect 3.2 — iOS certificate-removal instructions shown for an Android phone, contradicting the same card. *Verified on screen, reproduced.*

Immediately after pairing, the card said (screenshot 18):

> "Forgetting it here stops this Mac answering it, deletes the keys, and stops this Mac answering at its Tailscale name. **There is nothing to remove from your phone.**"

Closing and reopening the *same card* for the *same paired phone* gives (screenshot 19, reproduced
twice):

> "Forgetting it here stops this Mac answering it, and deletes the keys. It does **not** remove the certificate from your phone. To remove that too, on your phone open Settings, then General, then VPN and Device Management, then the RichOS profile, then Remove Profile."

Three things wrong at once: it contradicts what the same card said minutes earlier; it is an **iOS**
procedure on a card whose own first line reads "**Android phone** is paired"; and on the Tailscale path
no certificate was ever installed, which the flow itself told him. He would go hunting his Android
Settings for an iOS menu that does not exist, to remove something that was never there.

It looks like the home-only/self-signed copy being used as the default on reopen. The same bleed shows
on the phone — see Defect 4.2.

### Defect 3.3 — the pairing code expires in ~60 s and the whole flow silently resets. *Verified on screen.*

The code is shown with "This code lasts N more seconds", counting down from ~57. When it hits zero the
QR, the code and the six words **vanish with no message at all** and the dialog drops back to the "This
Mac is ready" screen (screenshot 17). Nothing says "that code expired, here is a new one."

That is exactly the person this feature is for: he reads the screen, walks to get his phone, unlocks
it, opens the camera — and comes back to a screen that looks like he imagined the whole thing. Sixty
seconds is not enough time for the task the screen is asking him to do.

### Defect 3.4 — "Use Rich from your phone" is not in Settings. *Verified on screen.*

The gear at the foot of the rail opens a panel containing: Your name · How much should Rich interrupt
you? · Technical view · Keep the stored output · Splash screen. That is the whole panel — I scrolled it
to its end (screenshot 10, and a page-down that moved nothing).

Everything else — **Theme, Text size, Techy Mode, Company, Home screen, Connected repositories, Account
connection, Memory folder, Use Rich from your phone, Updates** — lives behind a *second*, unlabeled
sliders icon at the top right. A person looking for a setting presses the gear. **Splash screen** appears
in **both** panels, which is how you can tell the split is not deliberate.

### Defect 3.5 — "Which entity is this work in?" *Verified on screen.*

Two problems in one small dialog: **"entity"** is schema vocabulary in front of a non-technical CEO
(everything else in the app says *company*), and the dialog is shown when there is exactly **one**
choice. Escape does close it correctly.

## 4 — The phone, on his Android. FAIL

*Verification basis: verified on the device. Frames taken only while the RichOS page was foreground,
checked with `mCurrentFocus` before each. Nothing pulled, installed, uninstalled or configured.*

### What works

- **No certificate warning.** The base address `https://mm1.tail770f6e.ts.net:8443/` loaded clean in
  Chrome — no interstitial, no "Your connection is not private", no profile prompt. The Mac promised
  exactly this and delivered it. Screenshot 21.
- **The six words matched** the Mac's, in order: *papaya nugget mosaic crayon orange figure*. Tapping
  "They match — pair this phone" paired it. Screenshot 22.
- **Both directions round-trip.** "hello from my phone" reached the Mac's thread; "banana", typed on
  the Mac, reached the phone. Screenshots 09, 25.
- **Unpair works, proven.** After "Forget this phone", reloading the phone gives "Your Mac isn't
  reachable from here." Screenshot 28.

### Defect 4.1 — the phone shows only Rich's side of the conversation. *Verified on the device, with a positive control.*

Neither the message typed **on the phone** nor the one typed **on the Mac** ever appears in the phone's
thread — only Rich's replies do. *Positive control:* the Mac's copy of the same thread shows "hello
from my phone" correctly (screenshot 09), so the message arrived; the phone simply does not render
user turns.

He is on the go, looking at a list of answers with no questions. He cannot see what he asked, cannot
tell which reply belongs to which message, and cannot confirm his message was sent. For the surface
whose whole purpose is being usable away from the desk, this is the most serious thing in the walk.
Screenshots 23, 24, 25, 28.

### Defect 4.2 — the phone talks about "the certificate your Mac made for itself" on the path that just promised no certificate. *Verified on the device.*

The verification card reads:

> "They are the name of **the certificate your Mac made for itself**. If they match what is on your Mac, this phone is talking to your Mac and to nothing else."

The Mac had just told him, in bold on the same flow: *"There is no certificate to install on this path.
If your phone asks you to install a profile, something is wrong — tell me."* A careful person reading
both concludes something **is** wrong, at the exact moment he is being asked to make a security
decision. Same home-only copy bleed as Defect 3.2. Screenshot 22.

### Defect 4.3 — the title breaks mid-word. *Verified on the device.*

The header renders **"Runni / ng"** — the thread title broken across two lines because three
items are crammed onto one row: the title, the word "Conversation", and a dropdown *also* reading
"Running". The first thing he sees after pairing is a broken word and the same word three times.
Screenshots 23, 24.

### Defect 4.4 — the empty state points at a button that is not there. *Verified on the device.*

> "Nothing here yet. Write to Rich, **or hold the button and speak**."

There is no such button on the phone page — the composer has a text field and "Send", nothing else.
Screenshot 23.

### Defect 4.5 — "Add to Home Screen" does not exist by that name on this phone. *Verified on the device.*

The Mac instructs: *"Add Rich to your phone's Home Screen and allow notifications when it asks."*
Chrome's menu on this phone offers **"Install and create shortcut"**. There is no "Add to Home Screen"
item. The good news is that the entry is offered at all, which means the manifest is being served
correctly; the instruction just names a control the device does not have. I did **not** install it —
that would put an app on his home screen, which is past "what pairing needs". Screenshot 27.

## 5 — Push with the app closed on Android. KNOWN GAP — not counted

*Verification basis: verified on the device up to the point named below; the remainder explicitly not
verified.*

The banner reads: *"Notifications are off, so Rich cannot reach you when this app is closed."* with
**"Turn on notifications"**.

Tapping it produced **two** prompts:
1. Chrome's site prompt — "mm1.tail770f6e.ts.net:8443 wants to send you notifications" — which I allowed.
2. Android's **system** prompt — "Allow **Chrome** to send you notifications?"

**I stopped at (2) and dismissed it.** That grants Chrome a system-wide notification permission on the
CEO's personal phone, which is a change to his device beyond "what pairing needs", and the brief draws
that boundary explicitly. So the Mac-side refusal wording — the thing the brief wanted recorded — was
**not reached, and I am not going to describe it from anywhere but the screen.**

**What would make it possible:** the same walk on a test Android device (or the emulator) where
granting Chrome the notification permission costs nobody anything; that reaches the subscribe call and
whatever `push.rs` says when it declines a non-Apple endpoint.

**What I can report, and it is good:** the client side handles refusal gracefully and honestly. The
banner updated itself to:

> "Notifications stayed off, so Rich can only reach you while this app is open."

Accurate, plain, no blame, no dead end. Screenshot 26. *Minor:* after the page was reloaded the banner
reverted to the generic "Notifications are off…" wording rather than keeping the better sentence.
