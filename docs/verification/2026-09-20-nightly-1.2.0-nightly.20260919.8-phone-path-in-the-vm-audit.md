# The phone path on nightly `.8`, walked end to end inside the test VM

**Verdict: READY FOR HIM.**

The defect that made `.7` unusable is gone, and it is not marginally gone. A sentence typed on the
phone reached the Mac in **181–635 ms** on five consecutive sends, against **30.67 / 54.47 / 48.79 s
and one send refused outright** on `.7`. Nothing was refused, nothing waited on a retry, and the
bubble was on the Mac's screen in **138–573 ms**.

| Step | | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phone → Mac, five sends: every one arrived, all under 1,000 ms to intake, none refused, none retried | **PASS** |
| 2 | The Mac says `It has reached this Mac…` and NOT `It is paired` until the phone confirms; `They do not match` leaves the Settings row quiet and the phone refused | **PASS** — with a real gap inside it, defect 1 |
| 3 | Sheet controls in the window at 1024×700; reopened sheet focuses a button; Settings quiet/live/paired; `Stop and go back` → port 8443 refused (exit 7) | **PASS** (4 of 5). **Mac → phone: NOT REACHED** — the guest has no engine, same as `.7` |

**The one thing this walk cannot tell him.** `Mac → phone` was not measured, here or on `.7`, because
a desk message in this guest is refused before it becomes a message at all (*"I can't take that on
yet — the RichOS engine isn't on this Mac"*). That is the **one-time human `claude /login` in the
guest** named in `docs/testvm.md`, not a product fault. Half the round trip is therefore proven and
half is not, and the half that is proven is the half he complained about.

## Verification mode

macOS 15 guest in the Tart test VM (`--vm ray8`), app window **1024×700**, one app window, Safari in
the same guest standing in for the phone, **side by side and never overlapping** (app at `(10,60)`
1024×700; Safari at `(1044,60)` 620×700 — 10 px of clear space between them). Two-user test: **yes**
— the Mac and the phone page are the two ends, and the same thread was open on both throughout.

Nothing was rendered, installed or launched on the host. The host's `pgrep -x richos-tauri` was empty
before the run and was never mine. No `pmset` but `-g` was not needed at all; no modifier keystroke,
no lock, no sleep, and the host's login keychain was never touched — the only keychain this walk
created lives inside the disposable guest (defect 5). No voice turn and no model turn anywhere.

Every claim below is **verified live on the guest's screen** unless it says otherwise. Contrast
ratios are computed from rendered pixels by `contrast.py`, never eyeballed.

**How the sheet was opened, since `.7` asked for it to be said:** a **synthetic mouse click at the
element's screen coordinates**, never `AXPress`. `AXPress` on this app's web-area controls does
nothing at all — I confirmed it again on `.8` against the `Not now` button, which returned without
acting. One further wrinkle found this time: a single click at a text field's coordinates lands ON
the field (System Events names it back) but leaves focus on the `AXWebArea`, so keystrokes go
nowhere. Typing therefore sets `AXFocused` through the accessibility API first. That cost one silent
no-op "send" before a composer-contents assertion caught it — see *The instrument* below.

## Step 1 — five sends, phone to Mac

`T_SEND` is the guest's clock at the instant the Send press is issued, `at` is the Mac's own
`intake.jsonl` timestamp, and both come off the **same clock inside the guest** with no ssh round
trip between them (see *The instrument*). Every figure uses the press instant **before** the click
call returns, which is the conservative end: the true press is somewhere between the two, so the real
numbers are these or smaller.

| 5 | `ray eight measured five` | 1789864018254.7 | 1789864018890 | **635.3 ms** | **573.1 ms** |

**Every send arrived. None showed `Not sent.`, none showed `One message could not be sent`, and none
waited on a retry** — the whole spread is 181–635 ms, far inside the outbox's first retry at 1 s, so
no retry can have fired. The phone's own label reads `Delivered.` on the newest message

last-message-status pattern rather than a missing state.

A sixth send (`ray eight send one`, the harness shakedown) is in the ledger at id 8 with **273.4 ms**

## Defects, ranked

### 1. HIGH — `They do not match` drops the credential but leaves the Mac answering, and never tells the person at the Mac

The device record is forgotten, the Settings row goes quiet and the phone is refused — that is the
security-critical half and it works. But **the Mac keeps its listener open on port 8443**, and the
sheet that is open in front of the person does not change at all.

Measured: after the press, the Mac answered `curl` on 8443 at **t+10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 s**, and
was still answering two minutes later when I closed the sheet by hand. Over that whole time the open
card still read `It has reached this Mac. Check that the six words on it are the six words below…`
with the six words underneath (`27-mac-after-mismatch.png`). The port only closed when I pressed
`Stop and go back` myself.

**The cause, with both sides of it.** The Mac-side `Forget this phone` calls
`PhoneChannel::forget()` (`richos/app/src-tauri/src/phone/mod.rs:962-975`), which does four things:

```rust
was.listener.stop();                    // stops answering on 8443
was.channel.devices.forget()?;          // drops the device record
self.hub.set_live(false);               // updates what the surface shows
ca::PhoneCa::forget(&self.data_dir, …)?; // deletes the CA key
```

The rejection path (`richos/app/src-tauri/src/phone/routes.rs:264-270`) does only the second:

```rust
eprintln!("[richos] the phone reported that the six words did NOT match; forgetting it");
if let Err(e) = channel.devices.forget() { … }
```

I confirmed the difference by walking both: `Forget this phone` took the port to `exit 7`
immediately; `They do not match` left it at `exit 0`.

**Why it matters beyond the plumbing.** The six words exist for exactly one scenario — something
other than his Mac is on the other end. The app's own comment says leaving it paired "in case he was
mistaken" is the one outcome worse than stopping. A person who has just pressed the alarm button
looks at his Mac and sees it still displaying the pairing card as though nothing was said. He has no
way to know the Mac heard him. Not blocking the publish — the credential really is gone — but it
should not reach him as "done".

### 2. MEDIUM — 120 seconds of `Getting this Mac ready...` when the keychain does not answer

The bound phonelive3 added is **real and it works**, and that is worth saying plainly: with a
`Keychain Not Found` dialog sitting over the sheet, the app waited, killed the child, and recovered
with a sentence I would happily ship —

> *"Your Mac's keychain did not answer, so I stopped waiting and nothing was stored. macOS may be
> holding a keychain window open behind this one — look for it, answer it, then press Set my phone up
> again."*

— and it re-enabled the button (`05-security-bound-fired.png`). Measured end to end at **~120 s**,
matching `TOOL_TIMEOUT` (`secrets.rs:173`), which I read before timing it rather than after.

The note is the length. Two minutes of a progress line that never changes is, to a user, a hang; most
people will have pressed something else or quit long before it resolves. The recovery sentence is
good enough that it deserves to be seen sooner — a first "this is taking longer than it should"
signal at ~10 s would cost nothing and would not weaken the 120 s bound.

### 3. LOW (carried, unfixed from `.7`) — the tailnet join loses a race with `tailscaled`

Reproduced exactly. `run.sh` attempted the join **9 s** after the guest reported an IP and got
*"failed to connect to local tailscaled … /var/run/tailscaled.socket: no such file or directory"*,
printed `tailnet=not-joined`, and carried on. A later `tailnet.sh join ray8` succeeded in 4 s. A
tester who misses that line walks the whole path against a Mac that cannot serve. The join should
wait for the daemon's socket, not for a fixed interval.

### 4. LOW (harness, new) — the join warns that pairing will fail, seconds before the certificate arrives

`tailnet.sh join ray8` printed *"WARNING — the daemon would not hand over a certificate; the app will
refuse to pair"* and exited. `tailnet.sh doctor ray8`, run immediately after, reported
`certificates: yes` and the app paired without trouble. The warning is checked too early; as written
it tells a tester to stop when nothing is wrong.

### 5. MEDIUM (harness, partly fixed from `.7`) — the fixture still has no usable keychain

`.7`'s defect 4 — `<fixture-home>/Library/Keychains` symlinked to the CEO's real keychain directory —
**is fixed**: it is now a real directory and `find . -type l -lname '/Users/alex*'` returns nothing.
Good.

It is now a real **empty** directory, so `/usr/bin/security` still finds no keychain and still raises
the `Keychain Not Found` dialog (`04-after-setup-press.png`). I created a throwaway keychain inside
the guest to finish the walk, and it then prompted for its password twice more, once per pairing
(`06-setup-retry.png`, `23-second-code-light.png`). On the CEO's Mac the login keychain exists and is
unlocked at login, so this is a fixture gap and not his problem — but it costs every future walk the
same detour. The fixture should ship a real, unlocked, empty login keychain.

### 6. LOW — small things, all carried from `.7` and all still true

* **The theme selector is three checkboxes, not radios.** AX reports `AXCheckBox|Light theme`,
  `AXCheckBox|Follow the system`, `AXCheckBox|Dark theme` for three mutually exclusive options.
* **`Paired with Phone` is a near-empty sentence** when the device reports a generic name.
* **Three "Not now" offers stack up at launch** before the home screen is usable.

## The instrument, and the one thing it caught

Timing a press against a repaint across ssh is a way to measure ssh. So the press and the capture
loop both run **inside the guest**, from one Python process, on one clock — `measure.py` starts a
`screencapture` loop, sleeps 1.2 s for a baseline, issues the click itself, and writes every frame's
timestamp beside the press instant. `intake.jsonl`'s `at` is on that same clock, which is what makes
leg 1 exact rather than sampled.

Sampling came out at a **40.8–47.6 ms median inter-frame gap** (396–528 frames per run). `.7` could
not get below ~170 ms; the difference is that the region here is the thread pane rather than the
whole window. So the brief's ~45 ms loop is reachable, and every leg-2 figure carries roughly ±45 ms.

Repaint detection is byte-identity of the captured PNGs. The region holds only the Mac's thread pane
— no clock, no pointer, no animation — so two frames of the same screen compress identically and a
hash change is a repaint. Each run showed exactly **3** distinct states, which is what a settled
screen looks like.

**And the thing it caught.** The first attempt at send 1 reported a clean measurement of a send that
never happened: a single synthetic click at Safari's composer activates the window without focusing
the field, the keystrokes went nowhere, and the Send press posted an empty box. The intake log was
the positive probe that exposed it — nothing arrived, and the composer was visibly empty
(`13-after-send1.png`). Every send after that asserts the composer's contents match the message
before a single frame is captured, and aborts if they do not. `.7`'s lesson — *a negative result
needs a positive probe* — arrived this time as a false **positive**, which is the more dangerous
direction.
