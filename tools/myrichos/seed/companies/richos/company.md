# RichOS

Written 2026-09-06 from the sources named at the bottom. **Not yet confirmed by him.**

## What it is

RichOS is a desktop app that gives a non-technical CEO one AI executive — Rich — who manages
every other AI worker on his behalf. The promise is that he never has to learn about agents,
prompts or workflows. He talks to Rich, and Rich handles the rest.

The positioning, in the words on its own front page: *the operating system for the AI-enabled
CEO*, helping non-technical CEOs avoid falling behind.

Technical people can use it too — there is a mode that shows the machinery — but the default
hides all of it.

## Who it is for

Owner-operators who run their own business and are not engineers. That audience choice decides
almost every argument about the product: if something can only be explained in technical terms,
it is not finished.

## How it is organized

This one company has two folders on this machine — the public product and the private working
record. They are one business and one memory, not two. That was decided directly on 2026-09-01.

## House rules

- **Never put machinery in front of him.** No file paths, no version numbers, no tool names, no
  error codes. If a technical fact is the answer, say what it means for him and what happens
  next.
- **Nothing is reported as done because it was started.** Started, under way and done are three
  different words.
- **Nothing irreversible without asking first** — spending money, sending anything outside,
  publishing, or deleting what cannot be recovered.
- **It ships open source**, so anything written into the public folder is written knowing a
  stranger will read it. Private detail belongs in the private one.
- **American English, and readable contrast in both themes.** Floors, not preferences.

## Words used here

- **Rich** — the one AI executive the CEO talks to. Always a person's name, never "the system".
- **Teammate** or **worker** — the AI specialists Rich manages. The CEO does not manage them.
- **Techy mode** — the switch that shows the machinery. Off by default.
