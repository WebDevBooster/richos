# Senior Judgment

Use this reference when the task needs senior design taste, not just mechanical checking.

## Start With Instinct

Look at the screen or flow and answer fast:
- What feels wrong before you can fully explain it?
- What feels cheap?
- What feels crowded, fake or filler-heavy?
- What feels too clever, too timid or too generic?
- What would a sharp user lose trust in immediately?

Do not suppress this reaction. Capture it, then explain it.

## Common Qualitative Failures

These are the kinds of failures that make a product feel second-rate even when nothing is technically broken:

- The page is a pile of components, not a coherent story
- The primary action is present but does not feel primary
- The layout is busy yet somehow says nothing important
- Data is visible, but not editorialized into meaning
- Empty states feel like implementation gaps
- Seeded states feel like raw database output
- The product tries to look rich by adding cards, not by clarifying value
- The screen uses strong visual language without enough informational discipline
- The interface sounds like software talking to itself, not a serious product speaking to a user

## Comparative Critique

Pick the strongest realistic benchmark for the moment:
- Strava for active daily tracking
- Whoop for serious data presentation
- Apple Health for calm information architecture
- Strong fintech products for trust, hierarchy and transaction clarity

Then ask:
- What would that product never allow on this screen?
- What would it simplify?
- What would it make more legible?
- What would it remove completely?

Never benchmark against weak competitors just to make the current work look acceptable.

## Taste and Restraint

Reject:
- filler widgets
- decorative complexity
- fake celebration
- fake urgency
- fake personalization
- dashboard sprawl
- gamification that feels juvenile
- over-explained interfaces that still are not clear

Strong product design is selective. It does not reward every possible data point with its own box.

## Trust Radar

Ask whether the experience feels:
- stable
- intentional
- serious
- discreet
- premium
- coherent

Anything that feels off-brand, overstuffed, confused or half-finished is a trust problem.

## Ruthless Prioritization

A senior review should isolate:
- the worst thing on the screen
- the most consequential trust problem
- the change that would most improve first-session confidence

Do not bury the important call under a long list of trivia.
